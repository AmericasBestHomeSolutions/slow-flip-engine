# Public Raise Paths — What We Can Legally Say and Do

> General legal information for planning, **not legal advice**. The attorney
> picks the path (Open Question OQ-2, `LANGUAGE-LIBRARY.md` §7) and blesses the
> language before anything ships.

## 1. The rule that doesn't move

**A promissory note is presumptively a security under federal law** (*Reves v.
Ernst & Young*), regardless of size. Three things follow:

- **Small doesn't mean exempt.** Many small notes from everyday people is the
  pattern state regulators watch most — promissory-note offerings are a
  perennial top enforcement category for state securities regulators (NASAA).
- **Labels don't help.** Calling it a "loan," "private lending," or a
  "partnership" changes nothing; regulators look at substance. Passive people
  giving money and relying on our efforts for a return = securities offering.
- **An "offer" happens in words, not at funding.** Publicly stating our rate
  plus an invitation to participate is an offer — even if no money moves, even
  if it's "just a webinar." Pre-offer hype can also be integrated into a later
  offering ("conditioning the market").

So the question is never "how small can the loans be" — it's **which exemption
lets us talk publicly.** There are real answers.

## 2. What we can do publicly RIGHT NOW (no exemption needed)

The education-only lane — legal today, and it's most of the Lonchando plan:

✅ Teach the category: what private lending is, how notes work in general,
financial literacy, the slow-flip method
✅ Build the brand: music, merch, mindset, community, events
✅ Tell our story and track record as a **business** (completed deals, what we
bought, what we did) — presented as history, not as an invitation
✅ Run **educational webinars** on the category
✅ Collect emails for an education list
✅ Invite people to conversations ("want to learn more? book a call")

❌ The moment content states our rate/terms **and** invites participation, or
solicits interest in investing with us specifically, we've left this lane.

## 3. The paths (attorney picks one — OQ-2)

### Path A — Regulation Crowdfunding (Reg CF): the fit for "small checks, public marketing"

This is the exemption built for exactly what Joe described: small amounts from
many everyday (non-accredited) people, with public promotion.

| Feature | Rule |
|---|---|
| Raise limit | Up to **$5M per 12 months** |
| Who can invest | **Everyone** — non-accredited investors capped per person (roughly: if income/net worth < $124K, the greater of $2,200 or 5%; if both ≥ $124K, 10%, max $124K/yr across all Reg CF deals) |
| How | **Must run through an SEC-registered funding portal or broker-dealer** — the portal hosts the offering, the disclosures, and the investor checkout |
| Paperwork | **Form C** filed with the SEC + financial statements (reviewed; audited above ~$618K unless first offering) + annual report |
| Public advertising | **"Tombstone" rule (Rule 204):** off-platform, we may publish only — that we're doing an offering, the terms as bare facts (amount, security type, price, closing date), factual business info, and a **link to the portal**. All persuasion, Q&A, and detail happens **on the portal's communication channels**, where Joe can answer publicly (identified as the issuer) |
| Testing the waters | **Allowed before filing** (Rule 206) — we can publicly gauge interest with required legends ("no money accepted, no offer until Form C is filed…") |

**What the funnel looks like under Reg CF:** Lonchando content (unrestricted
education) → educational webinar (category + track record) → "our offering is
live on [PORTAL] — full terms there" (tombstone language) → portal page does
the offer, checkout, and compliance. The 12% and terms live on the portal and
in the tombstone facts — and can even be teased pre-launch under testing-the-
waters legends.

**Costs of the path:** portal fees (commonly ~5–7% of the raise), Form C prep,
reviewed financials, annual reports, and discipline about the tombstone line.

### Path B — Intrastate / state crowdfunding

Most states have their own crowdfunding exemptions (built on federal Rule
147/147A): public advertising allowed **to residents of one state only**, with
state-set caps and filings. If Joe's investor base is one state, this can be
simpler and cheaper than Reg CF. Attorney checks [STATE]'s version.

### Path C — Stay private: the 506(b) relationship funnel (current RAISE-SYSTEM design)

No public offer, ever. Public education → genuine relationship, documented in
the CRM → private one-on-one offer. Two honesty checks the attorney will
enforce: the relationship must be **substantive** (two-way, with real
knowledge of the investor's situation — webinar attendance alone doesn't
count), and there should be **time and interaction** between meeting someone
and offering to them. A webinar designed as a conveyor belt into same-week
offers gets integrated into one non-exempt public offering.

### Path D — Reg A+ (later, at scale)

The "mini-IPO": up to $75M/yr, full public advertising, everyone can invest,
testing-the-waters allowed. Real audited-financials + SEC qualification costs.
This is the graduate-school version of Path A when El Doce outgrows $5M/yr.

## 4. The webinar, done legally (works under every path)

The educational webinar itself is fine **as long as it contains no offer**:

- Content: the category, the slow-flip method, a completed-deal walkthrough
  (history, not invitation), honest Q&A on how notes work generally
- **No slide with our rate + "here's how to get in."**
- The CTA depends on the chosen path:
  - Path A live offering → "full terms at [portal link]" (tombstone words only)
  - Path A pre-launch → testing-the-waters interest form with required legends
  - Path C → "book a one-on-one conversation" (starts the documented
    relationship; the offer comes later, privately)

## 5. Bright lines — the no-nos that never move

1. **Never state rate/terms + invitation publicly** outside an exemption that
   allows it (Reg CF tombstone/portal, TTW with legends, state crowdfunding,
   Reg A).
2. **Never accept a dollar** before the chosen exemption's paperwork is done
   (Form C filed and live on the portal; or signed private docs under 506(b)).
3. **Never "guarantee"** anything, anywhere (see LANGUAGE-LIBRARY §3).
4. **Never dress a security as something else** — "it's just a small loan,"
   "it's a gift with interest," "we're partners." Substance controls.
5. **Never take webinar-room commitments.** The room educates; offers happen
   on the portal or one-on-one.
6. **Never pay unlicensed finders** a cut for bringing investors.
7. **Every investor's state matters** — blue-sky laws apply where the investor
   lives (Reg CF preempts most of this; private paths don't).
8. **Keep the receipts** — every public post, webinar recording, and email is
   evidence of what was and wasn't an offer. Archive everything.
