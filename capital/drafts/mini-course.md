## Lesson 1

**Subject:** The Money Move Banks Hope You Never Learn
**Preview:** It's not a stock. It's not a scheme. It's older than both.

Hey [First Name],

Let's start simple. Private lending is not new. It's not a trend, not a crypto
coin, not a scheme with a countdown clock. It's one of the oldest deals in the
book. Somebody needs capital to do real work. Somebody else has capital sitting
still. The two of them shake hands.

That's it. That's the whole category.

Banks have run this play for a hundred years. Take your savings, pay you almost
nothing for the privilege, then lend that same money out at a much higher rate.
They keep the spread. You get a statement that says "thank you for your
patience."

Private lending flips the seat. Instead of the bank sitting in the middle, you
sit in the middle. You lend directly, through a written promissory note, to the
operator doing the work. You're not gambling on a ticker. You're not hoping a
founder's app goes viral. You're the bank. On paper. In writing. With your name
on the note.

## Lesson 2

**Subject:** What's Actually Written on the Note
**Preview:** Three questions every promissory note has to answer, no more, no less.

Hey [First Name],

Last lesson, private lending as a category. Today, the actual paper.

A promissory note is a written promise. Nothing mystical about it. It's one of
the oldest documents in commercial law, and every real one answers three
questions, in writing, before anyone signs:

1. How much money is changing hands.
2. What rate of interest gets paid, and how often.
3. When the principal, the original amount, comes back.

No verbal promises. No "trust me." The terms live on the page, dated, signed,
and legally binding on both sides.

Here's why this format matters more than people think. A note is not a stock
certificate and it's not a handshake. It doesn't move with the market. It
doesn't reprice because somebody had a bad earnings call. The terms you sign
are the terms you get, for the life of the note. That's the whole appeal of
the instrument: certainty, in writing, from day one.

It also means the note isn't casual. It's a real legal document, drafted with
an attorney, recorded the way real financial instruments get recorded. When we
walk through an actual note together, live, at a Lunch & Learn, you'll see the
real numbers, not a summary of them.

For now, just know the shape of the thing: amount, rate, schedule, term.
Everything else is detail.

Next lesson: how a slow flip real estate deal actually generates the money
that pays a note like this.

Slow money…

— Joe

## Lesson 3

**Subject:** Where the Money Actually Comes From
**Preview:** Not from thin air. From a very boring formula.

Hey [First Name],

Here's a question worth asking before you lend a dollar to anybody: where does
the money to pay you back actually come from?

With us, the answer is boring. Boring on purpose. That's the whole strategy.

We call it the slow flip, and it's the opposite of the flip you see on TV. No
auction-house drama, no 30-day sprint, no contractor racing a network deadline.
The slow flip is three steps, in order, every time:

**Buy right.** We don't chase a deal. We wait for the number to make sense
before we ever sign. La Escalera, the staircase, only works if every step is
the same height. No leaping, no guessing.

**Hold steady.** Once we own it, we do the unglamorous work: repairs, tenants,
management, time. This is the part most people skip past because it isn't
exciting. It's also the part that actually builds the value.

**Let time do the lifting.** Real estate that's bought right and held steady
tends to do what real estate does: pay rent, build equity, and appreciate.
That's the engine underneath every note we issue. The property does the work.
The note is just the paper that shares the result with the person who funded
it.

That spread, between what the property costs to hold and what it generates, is
where the note gets paid from. Not a promise. Not a projection on a slide. A
property, doing property things, on a schedule.

## Lesson 4

**Subject:** What Happens If Something Goes Wrong
**Preview:** The honest answer, in writing, before you ever need it.

Hey [First Name],

I'd rather answer this question before you ask it.

Every real lending relationship needs a real answer to: what protects me? Not
a vibe. Not "trust me." An actual answer, in the documents, that exists
whether things go well or don't.

Here's how we build that answer into every note:

<!-- LL-05 -->
Each note is secured by [SECURITY DESCRIPTION — e.g., a recorded lien on
specific real property / a personal guarantee / corporate assets — must match
the actual documents].

That's not marketing language. That's a legal position, recorded the way real
security interests get recorded, not something we describe after the fact.

And here's the part most pitches leave out, that we put in writing on purpose:

<!-- LL-10 -->
Private lending involves risk, including possible loss of principal. Notes are
not bank deposits and are not insured by the FDIC or any government agency.
Past performance does not guarantee future results.

<!-- LL-11 -->
Your investment is illiquid for the note term. Do not invest funds you may
need before the note matures.

We say that not to scare you off. We say it because a real answer includes the
downside, not just the upside. If we won't tell you the risk, don't trust us
with the return.

<!-- LL-32 -->
This is the certainty bucket, not the emergency fund — never invest money you
might need before the note matures.

<!-- LL-12 -->
This is not an offer to sell securities. Any offer is made only through the
note documents, to qualified investors, in accordance with applicable
securities laws.

This email is education, not an offer. Nobody's signing anything from a
mini-course. When you're ready to see the actual terms and the actual
protections on an actual deal, that conversation happens one-on-one, with the
real paperwork in front of you.

Last lesson: how people actually get started.

— Joe
