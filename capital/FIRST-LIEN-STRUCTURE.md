# The First-Lien Structure — One Lender, One Property, One Lien

> General legal information for planning, **not legal advice.** This document
> describes the lane; the attorney confirms it exists in [STATE] (OQ-3/OQ-4,
> `LANGUAGE-LIBRARY.md` §7) before anything ships.

## 1. The structure (as Joe defined it)

- Minimum loan: **$30,000**, one lender per property — never two
- Security: **first and only lien** on the specific slow-flip property,
  recorded mortgage/deed of trust in the lender's own name
- Coverage: property resold to an end buyer at roughly **3× the loan**
  (~$90K on a $30K loan), on payments
- Terms: **5-year note at 12%**, or a **4-year note at 9%** on properties
  where the end buyer's payment supports the faster amortization
- The lender is repaid from the end buyer's payment stream

## 2. Why this is different — and why it's not automatic

Under the Supreme Court's *Reves* "family resemblance" test, some notes are
**not** securities — and **a note secured by a mortgage on real estate is one
of the recognized non-security families.** Whether a specific note lands in
that family turns on four factors, in plain language:

| Factor | What helps us | What hurts us |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Why each side does the deal** | It's a commercial loan: lender gets interest secured by collateral they can foreclose on | Pitching it as "an investment," passive income, portfolio yield |
| **2. How it's distributed** | One-to-one negotiated loans, each tied to a specific property, a handful of known lenders | **Mass public advertising to strangers** — the more it looks like an offering to the public, the more it looks like securities distribution |
| **3. What the buyer reasonably thinks it is** | Documents, vocabulary, and conduct that all say *loan*: note + recorded mortgage, "lender," "borrower," foreclosure rights | Marketing that says *investment*: "returns," "investors," "opportunity," rate-forward ads |
| **4. Is there another scheme protecting them** | **The recorded first lien itself** — real, foreclosable collateral is exactly the "risk-reducing factor" *Reves* names | Pooling, fractionalizing, or unsecured variants that leave lenders relying only on us |

**The paradox to internalize:** the collateral (factor 4) is our strongest
fact — and the marketing style Joe described wanting (broad public enticement)
is the exact thing that flips factors 2 and 3 against us. The structure earns
the lane; **restrained marketing keeps us in it.**

Two more must-holds:

- **Never fractionalize.** Two lenders on one property = a fractional interest
  = securities treatment in most states, instantly. One lender, one property,
  always.
- **State law is its own gate.** Some states' blue-sky statutes sweep broader
  than *Reves*; some regulate arranging/brokering mortgage loans (e.g.,
  broker-license regimes for trust-deed lending); usury caps vary. The
  attorney answers this for [STATE] — see OQ-3.

## 3. How we market it (the discipline that keeps the lane)

### The public layer — Lonchando (unchanged, unrestricted)

Education about the category, the method, the track record, the community.
Public content can now also teach **"be the bank" private mortgage lending as
a category** — what a first lien is, how recording works, why collateral
matters, how business owners have lent this way for generations. Still no
rate + invitation, still no "here's how to get in."

### The bridge — how we reach Latino business owners (the target Joe named)

Relationship channels, not ad buys — culturally right *and* legally right,
because it keeps factor 2 (distribution) one-to-one:

- Chambers of commerce, merchant associations, church and community networks,
  referrals from existing lenders and vendors
- The Lunch & Learn, in Spanish where the room calls for it: education +
  track record + "if you'd like to see how a specific deal is structured,
  let's sit down"
- One-to-one conversations booked from webinars/events — never deal terms
  from the stage

### The private layer — the deal conversation (one-to-one, per property)

Terms are presented **for a specific property, to a specific person, in a
negotiated conversation**: this address, this purchase price, this appraisal
or comparable-sale support, this end-buyer contract, your name on the recorded
mortgage, lender's title policy, these payment dates. The person isn't
subscribing to an offering — they're deciding whether to make *this loan* on
*this collateral*.

## 4. Vocabulary shift (update flows into LANGUAGE-LIBRARY on attorney approval)

The words must match the substance — consistently, everywhere, because factor
3 is about what a reasonable person hearing us thinks they're getting:

| Say | Not |
|---|---|
| lender, private lender | investor |
| loan, note, first mortgage | investment, offering, opportunity |
| interest | returns, yield |
| "secured by a recorded first lien on [address]" | "protected," "more than protected," "can't lose" |
| "borrower" (us) | "sponsor," "issuer" |

**Specific correction:** "their money is more than protected" — never say
this. The lien is real and strong, and we describe it factually: *"Your loan
is secured by a recorded first and only lien on the property. If we fail to
pay, you have the right to foreclose."* Collateral is protection with a
process, not a guarantee: values move, foreclosure costs money and time, and
saying otherwise is the one habit that can undo the whole structure.

## 5. The mechanics checklist (every deal, no exceptions)

1. One lender, one property, one note — never pooled, never fractionalized
2. Mortgage/deed of trust **recorded** in the lender's name before or at
   funding; lender's title insurance policy issued
3. Loan-to-value documented (appraisal or comparable sales in the file)
4. The end-buyer contract structured so the lender's lien priority is clean —
   lien recorded ahead of the end buyer's contract interest (attorney designs
   this intersection; see OQ-4)
5. Payment schedule, default terms, and foreclosure rights stated in the note
6. Every deal file archived: who, what property, what conversation preceded it

## 6. The seller-financing side (separate compliance area — flag, don't skip)

Selling to end buyers "on payments" at ~3× is **seller financing**. If end
buyers are owner-occupants, Dodd-Frank / SAFE Act rules apply to us as the
seller-financer: possible licensed-MLO involvement, ability-to-repay
considerations, balloon restrictions, and state land-contract/contract-for-
deed rules (some states heavily regulate these). This doesn't touch the
lender-side analysis above, but it's the same properties — the attorney
reviews both sides together (OQ-4).
